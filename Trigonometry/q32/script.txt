In this problem, we have a building and a tower standing on the same level ground.


The building height is 20 mee-ter.


From the foot of the building, the angle of elevation to the top of the tower is 60 degrees.


And from the top of the building, the angle of elevation to the top of the tower is 30 degrees.


The question is asking us to find the height of the tower.


To make things easy, let the height of the tower be H, and let the distance between the building and tower be d.


What is the idea here?
<break time="0.5s" />


Both angles are looking at the same top point of the tower, so we can form two right-angled triangles and use ten values.


<break time="0.5s" />


First, let us use the 60-degree angle.


Here, opposite side is the tower height H, and adjacent side is d.


So,


tan 60 degrees is equal to H upon d


We know tan 60° is equal to root 3.


Hence,


H equal to root 3 times d


Got it? Perfect.


<break time="0.2s" />


Now look at the second angle, which is 30 degrees.


This time we are standing on top of the building.


The building is already 20 mee-ter tall.


So we are not seeing the full tower height.


We are only seeing the extra part above the building.


That extra height is H minus 20.


This is where many students make mistakes. We should not take H here. We should take H minus 20.


So,


tan 30 degree equals (H minus 20) upon d


And tan 30° is equal to 1 by root 3.


Hence,


(H minus 20) Upon d = 1 upon root 3


Now ,


From the first equation,


d equal to H upon root 3


Putting this value in the second equation and after solving this,


H minus 20 equal to H upon 3


After simplifying,


3H minus 60 equal to H


2H = 60


H = 30


<break time="0.6s" />


So the height of the tower is 30 mee-ter.


Hence, the final answer will be Option B, 30 mee-ter.
